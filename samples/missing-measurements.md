# Report missing measurements in a study

[Back to the exercises](README.md#applied-health-statistics) · [Leia em português](missing-measurements.pt.md)

In a fictional study, a report shows the day-30 means. Before comparing the groups, check how many people contributed to each mean.

| Group | Participants | Day-30 measurements available | Mean of available measurements |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| A | 20 | 18 | 12 |
| B | 20 | 12 | 10 |

[Open the fictional data (CSV)](data/missing-measurements.csv)

1. Calculate the number and proportion of missing measurements in each group.
2. Complete the sentence: “The mean of ___ was calculated from ___ of ___ participants in group ___. There were ___ missing measurements (___%).”
3. Before comparing the means, write one question you would ask about the missing measurements.

<details>
<summary>Compare with the suggested answer</summary>

For group A, the observed mean of 12 uses 18 of 20 participants. Two measurements are missing (10%). For group B, the observed mean of 10 uses 12 of 20. Eight measurements are missing (40%).

One question for the team is **why more measurements are missing in group B**. If missingness is related to the outcome, the difference between observed means can misrepresent the difference between the full groups.

</details>
